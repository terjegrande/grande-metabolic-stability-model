Title
The Liquidity–Friction Principle: A Unified Framework for Metabolic Stability Across Redox, Epigenetic, and Mitochondrial Timescales

Author Summary
Cells must remain flexible enough to respond to stress, yet stable enough to preserve identity. We introduce the Liquidity–Friction Principle, a mathematical and mechanistic model describing how metabolic liquidity (rapid molecular turnover) interacts with metabolic friction (epigenetic and structural constraints). This framework explains how cells maintain stability — and how they lose it in aging, cancer, and metabolic disease.

Introduction
Cells operate in a state of controlled disequilibrium. They must remain adaptable enough to respond to environmental and metabolic stressors, yet stable enough to preserve lineage identity and long‑term function. Traditional biological models treat metabolism, epigenetics, and redox biology as separate domains. However, increasing evidence suggests that these systems form a tightly integrated regulatory network.

We propose the Liquidity–Friction Principle, a unifying framework describing how cellular stability emerges from the interplay between:

Liquidity (L): the capacity for rapid molecular turnover

4. Model Predictions
The Liquidity–Friction model predicts:

High liquidity + moderate friction → optimal stability

High friction + low liquidity → pathological rigidity

High liquidity + low friction → chaotic drift

Low liquidity + low friction → metabolic collapse

These predictions align with observed patterns in aging, cancer, and metabolic disease.

Discussion
The Liquidity–Friction Principle provides a unified mathematical and mechanistic framework for understanding metabolic stability. It integrates redox biology, epigenetic regulation, and mitochondrial energetics into a single stability engine. The model explains how cells maintain identity across timescales and why certain pathological states emerge when liquidity or friction becomes imbalanced.
